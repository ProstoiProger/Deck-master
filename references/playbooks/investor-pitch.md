# Playbook: investor pitch

**Preconditions**: the audience can fund or buy; 8 to 12 minutes; the traction numbers exist and can be sourced.

## Steps

1. **The ask**: amount, use of funds, milestone it buys, and the date you want an answer. Write it first; the last slide repeats it.
2. **Problem**: who loses how much, with one sourced number from a study, an audit or your own data.
3. **Solution**: one line, three benefits, one screenshot; say why it is hard to copy.
4. **Market**: TAM, SAM, SOM with definitions; build SOM bottom-up (customers you can reach times price).
5. **Traction**: a funnel from leads to paying customers, or revenue by month; no vanity numbers.
6. **Model**: pricing plans with currency and period; show what drives revenue growth per customer.
7. **Competition**: a feature matrix with the two rows that decide a purchase; be honest about where others win.
8. **Plan and team**: three milestones the money buys; one proof point per person.
9. **Scaffold**: `new --event investor-pitch` (theme `venture-dark`; `startup-bright` for a bright room).
10. **QA**: every number has a `source:`; totals add up; forecasts are labelled as plans; lint, previews, timed run.

## Traps

A market size taken from a top-down report only; a funnel with an unexplained jump; hockey-stick forecasts presented as
results; a vague ask ("we are raising money"); a team slide with titles but no proof.

## Output

Deck source, `.pptx`, `.pdf`, speaker script, an appendix of backup slides for questions (unit economics, cohort data).
